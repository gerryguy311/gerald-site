Full introduction

About 45 seconds

Every weekday morning at 8am Eastern, four hundred people show up live to hear one person walk through what happened in cybersecurity in the last twenty four hours, and what it actually means for their job. Another two thousand watch the replay before the day is out.

That has now happened 1,232 weekday mornings in a row.

Not a campaign. Not a product launch. Just showing up, every single day, for years. And somewhere along the way it stopped being a show and became a community. Seventeen thousand people in a Discord server. A quarter of a million more on YouTube. And a long list of practitioners who will tell you they got their first job in this field because of something that started on that livestream.

He has a PhD in cyber operations, two decades in the industry, and a SANS award for the show. Those are real, but they're not why he's up here.

He's up here because he answered a question most people only ask themselves once: what actually happens if you just don't stop?

Speaking

I don't have a circuit talk that I give everywhere.

Most of my speaking comes from people asking, and almost all of it is built for the specific room. I'd rather spend a call understanding who is in your audience and what would actually move them, then build something that serves them, than hand you a deck I've already given forty times. The goal every time is the same: entertaining and educational, in that order, because nobody learns anything from a talk they tuned out of.

Themes I keep coming back to

Consistency, and why cyber programs actually fail

Most security programs don't fail on strategy, they fail on execution and follow-through. What it takes to build something that still works in year three, drawn from running a daily program with zero missed days.

Supply chain and third-party risk

Where the real exposure sits, how to size it without a hundred-question spreadsheet nobody reads, and what leadership actually needs to decide. Delivered to state agency CIOs.

The modern threat landscape, for people who aren't in security

What a general workforce or executive team genuinely needs to understand right now, without fear-mongering and without jargon. Delivered to enterprise staff and full workforces.

GRC as a career, and how people actually get in

The path into governance, risk, and compliance, what the work is really like, and what hiring managers are looking for. This is the subject I've taught to more students than any other.

Community as security infrastructure

Why the defenders' advantage is collective, and what it takes to build a room where people share what they know instead of guarding it. The subject of my Wild West Hackin' Fest keynote.

Long

287 words

Gerald Auger, PhD is the founder of Simply Cyber, a cybersecurity education and media company built on one deeply unglamorous habit: showing up.

Since December 2021 he has hosted the Daily Cyber Threat Brief live every weekday morning, walking practitioners through the last twenty four hours in security and what it actually means for their work. The show won the SANS Difference Makers Award for Podcast of the Year in 2024. It has now run for 1,232 consecutive weekday mornings without missing one, drawing roughly four hundred live attendees each morning and another two thousand on replay the same day.

What began as a livestream became a community. Simply Cyber reaches more than 275,000 subscribers on YouTube and 75,000 followers on LinkedIn, and its Discord server holds 17,000 members who trade job leads, interview prep, and hard-won advice with each other every day. Gerald built the whole thing around three values he will not trade away: support, inclusion, and empowerment.

He holds a PhD in cyber operations from Dakota State University and has spent more than twenty years in the field, concentrated in governance, risk, and compliance. He is an adjunct professor at The Citadel and Director of Strategy at Kairos Sec. Through Simply Cyber Academy he has taught GRC to more than 11,000 students, most of them through the Definitive GRC Analyst Master Class, priced deliberately low because the point is access rather than margin.

His work lives at the intersection of two things this industry usually keeps apart: technical rigor and plain human language. He is at his best explaining why a risk matters to someone who did not ask for a lecture, whether that is a room of executives, a first-time analyst, or four hundred people in a live chat at eight in the morning.

He lives in Charleston, South Carolina.
